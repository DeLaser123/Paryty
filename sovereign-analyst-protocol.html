1. Executive Summary & Philosophy

1.1 The Core Thesis

The Dollar Index (DXY), Gold (XAU/USD), and Oil (WTI/Brent) form a coupled tripartite system governed by macro regime states, institutional flows, and cross-asset causal dynamics. Peer-reviewed research establishes that:

  • Granger causality runs from DXY to both Gold and Oil (Zhang et al., Economics Bulletin; MPRA Paper 31795). The dollar statistically precedes commodity price movements.
  • Simultaneous equation models confirm oil prices are significantly affected by gold, the USD exchange rate, and equity markets concurrently (Baffes & Hossain, MPRA Paper 70452).
  • Regime-switching dynamics govern gold and commodity behavior — a single static model fails across different market states (Hamilton, 1989; IJFR, 2021).
  • Real yields (10Y TIPS) have a documented strong inverse relationship with gold prices (University of Malta, 2025; PIMCO, 2025).
  • Cross-asset correlation breakdowns cluster around crisis events and regime transitions (JDACM, 2025).

1.2 The Irreducible Edge

Every data source and formula in this protocol is publicly available. The un-replicable component is Layer 6: your personal synthesis engine — the way you weight conflicting signals, resolve ambiguities between layers, and assign conviction scores. This is a cognitive skill developed through deliberate repetition, not a purchased tool.

1.3 Design Principles

  • Free data only: No Bloomberg, no Reuters Eikon, no premium feeds.
  • Manual-first: Every step is executable with pen, paper, spreadsheet, and a browser.
  • Programmable-later: Layers 1–5 contain rule-based logic suitable for future software encoding.
  • Regime-aware: No single strategy applies in all conditions. The framework adapts.
  • Research-grounded: Every formula and threshold traces to peer-reviewed literature.

3.2 Regime Classification Formula

Compute the Regime Score from four standardized indicators. Each indicator is scored 0–3 based on thresholds derived from Hamilton’s two-state model (high-vol/low-vol) extended to four states via quantile-based classification:

VIX Level Score:
  0 = VIX < 15  (complacency)
  1 = 15 ≤ VIX < 20  (normal)
  2 = 20 ≤ VIX < 30  (elevated fear)
  3 = VIX ≥ 30  (stress/crisis)

VIX Term Structure Score:
  Contango (VIX futures > spot) = 0 (normal)
  Flat = 1
  Backwardation (VIX futures < spot) = 2–3 (fear is NOW, not future)
    Mild backwardation (<5% inversion) = 2
    Deep backwardation (≥5% inversion) = 3

Yield Curve Score (10Y-2Y spread):
  0 = Spread > +100bps  (steep curve, early cycle)
  1 = +25bps to +100bps  (normal)
  2 = -25bps to +25bps  (flattening, late cycle)
  3 = Spread < -25bps  (inverted, recession signal)

Credit Spread Score (HY OAS):
  0 = OAS < 350bps  (tight, benign)
  1 = 350–500bps  (normal)
  2 = 500–700bps  (stress)
  3 = OAS > 700bps  (crisis/distress)
Composite Regime Score = VIX_Score + VIX_Term_Score + Yield_Score + Credit_Score

Range: 0–12

Classification:
  0–3  → R1: Risk-On Expansion
  4–6  → R2: Risk-On Late-Cycle
  7–9  → R3: Risk-Off Contraction
  10–12 → R4: Crisis / Liquidity Shock

4. Layer 2 — Structural Flow Analysis

Research basis: BIS Working Paper 828 on currency composition of FX reserves; IMF COFER data; WisdomTree (2025) on central bank gold vs. Treasury holdings; CFTC COT report literature.

4.1 COT (Commitment of Traders) Analysis

The CFTC publishes weekly positioning data every Friday, showing where different trader categories stand as of Tuesday’s close. This is the closest free proxy to institutional positioning available.

4.1.1 Key COT Metrics

Net Position = Long Contracts − Short Contracts

For each instrument (DXY futures, Gold futures, WTI futures):
  Commercial Net Position = Commercial Longs − Commercial Shorts
  Non-Commercial Net Position = Non-Comm Longs − Non-Comm Shorts

COT Index (Williams, 1987):
            Current Net Position − Min(Net Position, N weeks)
COT_Index = −−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−− × 100
            Max(Net Position, N weeks) − Min(Net Position, N weeks)

Where N = 156 weeks (3 years) or 260 weeks (5 years).
Range: 0 to 100.

4.1.2 COT Index Interpretation

COT Index (Non-Commercials)SignalAction Bias
> 80Speculators extremely net longBEARISH — crowded long, reversal risk
60–80Speculators moderately longNEUTRAL
20–60Speculators balancedNEUTRAL
0–20Speculators extremely net shortBULLISH — crowded short, squeeze potential
Key Insight: The divergence between commercial and non-commercial COT Index is more powerful than either alone. When Commercials COT Index > 60 (they are buying) while Non-Commercials COT Index < 20 (specs are selling), this is the strongest contrarian bullish signal — smart money is accumulating while the crowd is panicking.

4.2 Central Bank Reserve Flows

The IMF COFER data reveals how central banks allocate reserves between USD and gold. The historic 2025 shift where central banks held more gold than US Treasuries for the first time represents a structural dollar de-accumulation trend that overrides traditional real-yield signals for gold.

Central Bank Gold Demand Intensity:
  Track quarterly central bank net purchases (tonnes) from World Gold Council
  If quarterly purchases > 200 tonnes → STRUCTURAL GOLD BID (overrides real yield headwind)
  If quarterly purchases < 100 tonnes → Gold must rely on other drivers

USD Reserve Share Trend:
  COFER USD share declining QoQ → STRUCTURAL USD BEARISH
  COFER USD share stable/rising → USD supported by reserve demand

5. Layer 3 — Cross-Asset Correlation State

Research basis: JDACM (2025) cross-asset correlation shifts in crises; MPRA (2025) DXY-gold-oil interdependencies; MDPI (2025) transfer entropy causal networks with 240-day rolling windows.

5.1 The Correlation Matrix

Maintain a rolling Pearson correlation matrix computed from daily log returns. The research literature uses rolling windows of 60 days (3 months) for tactical analysis and 240 days (1 year) for structural analysis (MDPI, 2025).

Daily Log Return:
  r_t = ln(P_t / P_{t-1})

Rolling Pearson Correlation (60-day window):
                         Σ[(r_X,i − r̄_X)(r_Y,i − r̄_Y)]
  ρ(X,Y) = −−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−
                         √[Σ(r_X,i − r̄_X)²] × √[Σ(r_Y,i − r̄_Y)²]

Where i ranges over the most recent 60 trading days.

Compute for all pairs:
  ρ(DXY, Gold), ρ(DXY, Oil), ρ(Gold, Oil)
  ρ(DXY, US10Y), ρ(Gold, US10Y), ρ(Gold, SPX)
  ρ(Oil, SPX), ρ(Gold, Copper)

5.3 Correlation Velocity (Rate of Change)

Beyond the static correlation value, track how fast correlations are changing:

Correlation Velocity = ρ_current − ρ_4weeks_ago

If Δρ > +0.3 in 4 weeks for any pair → RAPID CORRELATION SHIFT (regime stress)
If Δρ < −0.3 in 4 weeks → CORRELATION UNRAVELING (regime stress)

7.2 Volume Profile Analysis

Using TradingView’s free Volume Profile indicator on daily charts:

Point of Control (POC): The price level with the highest traded volume in the lookback period
  → Institutions accumulate at POC. Price tends to revert to POC when directionless.

High Volume Nodes (HVN): Price levels with above-average volume concentration
  → Act as support/resistance. Price slows and consolidates at HVNs.

Low Volume Nodes (LVN): Price levels with below-average volume
  → Price accelerates through LVNs. These are "thin" areas where moves are fast.

Value Area (VA): The price range containing 70% of the total volume
  Value Area High (VAH) = upper boundary → resistance
  Value Area Low (VAL) = lower boundary → support

Lookback Periods:
  Intraday scalps: Previous day’s volume profile
  Day trades: Previous week’s volume profile
  Swing trades: Previous month’s volume profile

7.3 Economic Event Calendar Protocol

Before each session, identify all high-impact events from ForexFactory or Investing.com. The protocol for each event:

Pre-Event (T-30 min):
  Reduce position size by 50% or close intraday positions entirely.
  Record pre-event levels: DXY, Gold, Oil, VIX.

Event Execution (T+0 to T+5 min):
  DO NOT TRADE. This is where spreads blow out and stops get hunted.
  Observe the initial reaction direction and magnitude.

Post-Event (T+5 to T+30 min):
  If the reaction aligns with your Layer 1–4 thesis → Enter on the first pullback.
  If the reaction contradicts your thesis → Stand aside, reassess.
  If the reaction is muted (priced in) → The market has already moved; wait for next catalyst.

8.1 The Synthesis Protocol

Every trading day, after completing Layers 1–5, execute this 5-step synthesis:

Step 1: Determine the Dominant Layer

Not all layers carry equal weight at all times. Use this regime-dependent weighting:

LayerR1 (Expansion)R2 (Late-Cycle)R3 (Contraction)R4 (Crisis)
L1: Regime15%20%25%35%
L2: Structural Flows25%25%20%15%
L3: Correlation State15%20%20%15%
L4: Positioning/Sentiment25%15%15%15%
L5: Microstructure/Timing20%20%20%20%

In R1/R2, positioning extremes (L4) and flows (L2) matter most. In R3/R4, regime state (L1) dominates everything else because macro forces overwhelm individual positioning.

Step 2: Identify Conflicts Between Layers

Layers will frequently disagree. This is not a flaw — it is a feature. Document every conflict explicitly:

CONFLICT LOG:
  Conflict 1: [Layer X says BULLISH] but [Layer Y says BEARISH]
    Resolution: [Your reasoning for which layer you trust more in THIS context]
    Confidence in resolution: [HIGH|MEDIUM|LOW]

  Conflict 2: ...
  (Repeat for all conflicts)
The Conflict Resolution Rule: When Layer 1 (Regime) conflicts with any other layer, Layer 1 wins in R3 and R4 regimes. In R1 and R2, the conflict is genuine and requires your judgment. The way you resolve these conflicts is the core of your un-replicable edge.

Step 3: Assign Conviction Score

Conviction Score (1–5):
  5 = All 5 layers aligned, no conflicts, strong signal in each
  4 = 4 of 5 layers aligned, minor conflict in one
  3 = 3 of 5 layers aligned, moderate conflicts
  2 = Mixed signals, no clear dominant thesis
  1 = Layers broadly disagree, no actionable setup

Minimum Conviction Threshold:
  Intraday trades: Conviction ≥ 3 (shorter holding period needs less certainty)
  Day trades: Conviction ≥ 4 (holding through a session requires stronger thesis)
  DO NOT TRADE at Conviction ≤ 2

8.2 The Synthesis Engine Is Not a Formula

The tables and protocols above are the scaffolding. The engine itself is:

  • Your ability to look at a COT divergence and feel whether it matters in the current regime.
  • Your willingness to override a “textbook” setup because something doesn’t smell right.
  • Your pattern recognition for how DXY moves 20 minutes before Gold responds.
  • Your discipline to stand aside when conviction is low, even after days of not trading.
  • Your post-trade honesty in the review log.

This is why a Bloomberg terminal and a team of analysts cannot replicate your edge. They have better data but not your synthesis.
